In short

  • Your prayers stay on your phone. We never get to see what you log.
  • Vakat needs no account; an account is optional (section 4b). There are no ads and no ad tracking. We only evaluate how the setup and the subscription page are used, which subscriptions there are and, if you tell us, your age group and how you heard about Vakat, without any prayer data (sections 8, 9 and 9a).

2. What stays on your phone

Vakat stores the following only on your device. It is not sent to us, and we have no access to it:

  • your prayer log: logged prayers, pauses and your streak
  • the days you fasted in Ramadan
  • “Learn to pray”: your progress and the school of thought (madhhab) you chose
  • saved verses
  • your name (optional), your goal, your city, the calculation method, reminders, alarms and other settings

Prayers, pauses (for example during your period, illness or travel), fasting and your school of thought can reveal something about your religion and your health. The GDPR counts such information among the special categories of personal data (Art. 9 GDPR). That is why it does not leave your device. The only exception is your light in the Circle, if you use it (section 4).

In “Me” → “Delete all prayer data” you remove all logged prayers, pauses and your streak. When you delete Vakat, iOS deletes the app’s data on your device. Only the Circle’s anonymous ID stays in the keychain, if you used the Circle (section 4.5). On Android, Android deletes all of the app’s data together with Vakat, including the anonymous ID.

If you use iCloud Backup or back up your iPhone to a computer, iOS includes this data in the backup, as it does for every app. We have no access to it. On Android, Vakat puts nothing into the backup with Google; only if you transfer data directly from device to device when switching to a new phone can Android take it along.

3. Location and map services

Vakat calculates prayer times on your device from the city you choose. City, coordinates and time zone stay on your device.

  • Use my location: iOS asks for your permission first, only for while you are using Vakat. Vakat determines your location once and asks Apple Maps which place it is; for this, the position is sent to Apple. Otherwise Vakat does not request your location, not in the background either; the only exception is the Qibla compass (below). Vakat only asks for your approximate location; prayer times and the compass need no more.
  • City search: What you type into the search is sent to Apple so that suggestions appear. During setup, Vakat also searches Apple Maps for the city of your time zone (for “Europe/Paris”, Paris) to suggest it to you.
  • Globe during setup: Once you choose your city, a globe from Apple Maps turns to it and shows the way to Mecca. For this, Vakat loads map images of that area from Apple.
  • Mosques nearby: Vakat sends the area around your city (about 20 kilometres) and the word “mosque” to Apple Maps.
  • Qibla compass: Vakat calculates the direction from your city and turns it with your device’s compass. If you allowed location access, the compass uses your approximate location while it is open, to find true north. Nothing is sent or stored.

Apple processes these requests under its own privacy policy. We receive nothing from it. The legal basis is the feature you use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

On Android, Android asks for your permission before “Use my location”, only for your approximate location and only while you are using Vakat. Your phone’s Google Play services determine the location. For the place name of your location, the city search, the suggestion from your time zone and mosques nearby, Vakat uses your phone’s location service (geocoder), on most devices Google’s; the position, the search term, or the area around your city and the word “mosque” are sent to this service. Vakat draws the globe during setup from NASA images of the earth (Blue Marble and Black Marble) that come with the app; nothing is loaded for it. As on the iPhone, the Qibla compass uses your approximate location only while it is open; Vakat sends and stores nothing in the process. Google processes these requests under its own privacy policy; we receive nothing from it.

4. The Circle

4.1 Optional, and only with your consent

In the Circle, you see whose light is lit for Fajr today. The Circle is optional. Before anything is shared, Vakat asks for your explicit consent, because a prayer circle reveals something about your faith (Art. 6(1)(a) and Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). Until then, no Circle data goes to the server. Vakat stores when you consented only on your device. Without the Circle, everything else in Vakat works just the same.

4.2 What is stored

About you:

  • your name in the Circle: your choice, up to 20 characters, a nickname is enough
  • your light: if you logged the Circle’s prayer, Vakat stores until when your light is lit, namely until the next Fajr, rounded down to the full hour (field litUntil). Otherwise the field is empty. Whether you prayed on time or made it up later is not stored there.
  • the code you joined with, and when you joined
  • an anonymous ID (Firebase user ID), without a name, email address or phone number

About the Circle: its name (“Fajr Circle”), the Circle’s prayer, a challenge if one was chosen (30 or 40 days and the start day), the invitation code, when it was started, who started it and who belongs to it, each as an anonymous ID.

Nudges (“… is thinking of you at Fajr”): from whom, to whom, on which day and when.

Never shared: your prayer log, other prayers, pauses, missed prayers, fasting, your city and your location. “Not logged”, “no” and “Paused” look the same in the Circle: the light is off.

4.3 Who sees it, and why

This information is used only to make the Circle work. Only the members of your Circle can see it (up to 8 people). Nudges are seen only by the sender and the recipient. Whoever started the Circle can remove people and disband the Circle.

4.4 Provider and storage location

The Circle runs on Firebase by Google. Google processes the data on our behalf as a processor; the Firebase Data Processing and Security Terms apply.

  • The Circle data is stored in Cloud Firestore in Frankfurt am Main, Germany (region europe-west3). Vakat talks to Firestore directly over its encrypted interface (HTTPS).
  • For the anonymous sign-in, Vakat uses Firebase Authentication. This service runs only in data centres in the US. In the process, Google also processes your IP address and device information (user agent) to prevent abuse, and keeps IP addresses for a few weeks. The transfer to the US is based on the European Commission’s adequacy decision for the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, under which Google is certified.
  • Circle data never goes to Google Analytics (section 9). Vakat does not use Crashlytics or Cloud Messaging.

More from Google: Privacy and security in Firebase.

4.5 Leaving and deleting

You find everything in the Circle under “More”:

  • Leave Circle deletes your entry in the Circle (name, light, code and when you joined) and your nudges. If you are the last person to leave, the Circle is disbanded. If you started the Circle and leave, the member who has been in it the longest takes over.
  • Disband Circle (only whoever started it) deletes the Circle for everyone, with all names, lights and the code.
  • Remove from Circle (under “Members”, only whoever started it) deletes that person’s entry.
  • Withdraw consent: you leave the Circle, Vakat deletes your anonymous ID at Firebase and forgets your consent. After that, nothing more is sent to the server.
  • Nudges are deleted by Vakat as soon as you dismiss them, otherwise after one to two days, when either of you opens the Circle again.

Firebase keeps your anonymous ID until you withdraw your consent. If you delete Vakat without leaving the Circle, your name stays in the Circle; your light goes out by itself. Your name stays until the Circle is disbanded or the person who started it removes you. The ID stays in your device’s keychain: if you reinstall Vakat and consent again, you find your Circle again and can leave it. On Android, the anonymous ID is lost with the app; without an account (section 4b) you will not find the Circle again. Or write to us and we will delete your entry. To do so, tell us your name in the Circle and the code.

4.6 Hiding and reporting

Long-press a name in the Circle: Hide name no longer shows that name and its nudges on your device; which names you hide is stored only on your device. Report opens an e-mail to us with that name and the Circle code; you decide whether to send it. We use it only to look into the report and to reply to you (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR), and delete it once the matter is settled.

4a. Likes on verses

Your heart on a verse stays on your device. At your first like, Vakat asks whether it should be counted anonymously. Only if you choose “Count anonymously” does Vakat also count your likes on the server, so that you and others can see how many people like a verse. Cloud Firestore in Frankfurt am Main (europe-west3) then keeps only one number per verse, not who liked it; each like changes it by one. For this, your device signs in with the same anonymous ID as for the Circle (Firebase Authentication, section 4.4). Because a like on a verse of the Quran can reveal something about your faith, the legal basis is your explicit consent (Art. 6(1)(a) and Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). Under “Privacy” in the app you withdraw it with a switch; Vakat then takes your likes back off the numbers and sends nothing more. If you choose “Only on my phone”, nothing goes to the server and you see no numbers.

4b. Account (optional)

An account is optional; Vakat works without one, with “Skip” at the first start or never later. If you sign in with Apple, Google or your email address, Firebase Authentication (section 4.4) stores your email address, an identifier and, if Apple or Google pass it on, your name; with an email address also your password, only in encrypted form. With “Sign in with Apple” you can hide your email address. “Continue with Google” opens Google’s sign-in page; Google’s privacy policy governs what Google processes there.

With an account, your Circle and your likes stay, even on a new device: the Circle is tied to the same identifier as the anonymous one before, and Vakat keeps your likes as a list of verses in Cloud Firestore in Frankfurt am Main (europe-west3) (users/{identifier}) that only you can read. If you chose “Only on my phone” for likes, Vakat does not keep them. Your prayer log, pauses, fasting and your settings stay on your device. The account never goes to Google Analytics or RevenueCat.

Because an account with a prayer app can reveal your faith, the legal basis is the explicit consent you give by signing in (Art. 6(1)(a) and Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR). In the app under “Me” → “Account” you can sign out or delete the account; Vakat then deletes the account, your place in the Circle and the kept likes. Signing out alone deletes nothing.

On Android, Vakat offers sign-in with Google and with your email address; “Sign in with Apple” is not available there at the moment. “Continue with Google” opens Google’s sign-in page in the browser. Everything else in this section applies the same way.

Deleting your account without the app: You can also request the deletion of your account by email, on iPhone and Android: write to david.zwirtmayr@dopaminedept.org, ideally from your account’s email address (with “Continue with Google”, that of your Google account). We will then delete your account at Firebase Authentication, your kept likes and your place in the Circle within one month and confirm it to you by email. We cannot reach data that is only on your device; you delete it in the app or together with the app. The subscription statistics and the usage analysis are not tied to your account but to random IDs (sections 8 and 9).

5. App Lock

App Lock uses Apple’s Screen Time, for yourself. You choose which apps are locked in a window provided by Apple. Vakat only receives placeholders (tokens) for this and does not learn which apps or websites you choose or how long you use them.

So that the lock knows when to let you back in, Vakat keeps the following in a shared storage area on your device (App Group): your lock settings, which prayers of the days around today are logged, whether you have just postponed the lock, and whether a pause is on. Only Vakat and its extensions read this storage. Your prayer log is not in it, and none of it leaves your device.

If you choose the prayer check or a photo, for example of your prayer mat, to unlock, the button on the lock opens Vakat through a notification. There the camera recognizes the first three steps of your prayer, or your iPhone compares your photo with the setup photo (see section 7). Which motif you chose is part of your lock settings; the photos themselves are not in the shared storage.

On Android there is no Screen Time for other apps. There Vakat shows you the apps with an icon on your phone to choose from; which ones you choose, Vakat stores only on your device. For this you give Vakat three permissions in Android’s settings: “Usage access”, so that Vakat can see during a lock which app is in front; “Display over other apps”, so that the lock appears over a chosen app; and “Alarms & reminders”, so that the lock starts and ends on time. While a lock is on, a Vakat service runs in the foreground, and Android shows a silent notification for it. From the usage data Vakat reads only which app came to the front last, only during a lock and only while the screen is on. Vakat does not store this, does not evaluate how long you use apps and sends none of it. Vakat keeps the note about prayers, postponing and pauses in the app’s own storage on your device, which only Vakat reads. The prayer check and the photo to unlock work as in section 7.

6. Widgets and shortcuts

Widgets, the Live Activity, Siri and Shortcuts, and the button in Control Center run on your device. From the shared storage they read only what they need to display: city, calculation method, your time adjustments, the language and whether the current prayer is already logged or a pause is on. If you log a prayer there, it is noted in the shared storage, and Vakat adds it to your prayer log the next time you open the app. Vakat updates the Live Activity only on the device, without a push server. If you use Siri, Apple processes your voice requests under its own terms.

On Android, these are the “Next Prayer” and “Verse of the Day” widgets, the “Current prayer” notification with “Log”, the “Log Prayer” tile in Quick Settings, the app icon shortcuts (long press) and “Log” in reminders. They run on your device and read only the same information as above from a shared storage area of Vakat. If you log a prayer there, Vakat notes it in this storage and adds it to your prayer log the next time you open the app. Vakat updates the “Current prayer” notification only on the device, without a push server.

7. Notifications, alarms, photos and sharing

  • Reminders and the Fajr alarm are local iOS notifications and alarms. They are scheduled on your device, without a server. This also applies to the reminder before your free trial ends. On Android, they are local Android notifications and alarms, also without a server.
  • Camera (photo task, App Lock photo and prayer check): Vakat only uses the camera when you switch it on. For the Fajr alarm’s photo task and for unlocking the App Lock with a photo, you take one photo of what you chose, for example your prayer mat. It stays on your device only, not in backups; if the alarm and the App Lock have the same motif, they use the same photo. It is deleted as soon as neither the alarm nor the App Lock needs it. Your iPhone compares the morning photo or the photo to unlock your apps with Apple’s image recognition directly on the device and then discards it; it is never stored. For the prayer check of the App Lock and the Fajr alarm, your iPhone reads only your posture from the camera picture (takbir, standing, rukuʿ); nothing is recorded or stored. None of this is sent.
  • Camera on Android and ML Kit: On Android, the setup photo is kept in the app’s own storage, not in backups, and is deleted as described above. Your phone compares the morning photo or the photo to unlock your apps with Vakat’s own image comparison directly on the device and then discards it; it also recognizes a sink with the image labeling of ML Kit, a Google library that runs on the device. For the prayer check, your phone reads your posture with ML Kit’s pose detection, also on the device; nothing is recorded or stored. Pictures and postures never leave your device. ML Kit does, however, send usage and performance data to Google (Google LLC): device manufacturer and model, Android version, app name and version, a random per-installation ID, how long the recognition took, image format and size, and error codes. Google uses this data to run ML Kit, find bugs and improve it, transfers it encrypted and does not pass it on to third parties (Google’s disclosure); the transfer to the USA is based on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. If you use these features, this cannot be turned off in Vakat. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in image recognition that runs on the device instead of sending pictures (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
  • Photos: Vakat only asks for permission to add photos, so that you can save cards and verses as images or videos to your Photos. Vakat cannot see your photos. On Android, Vakat saves images and videos to your gallery, from Android 10 without a permission, before that with the storage access Android asks for. Vakat does not read your photos there either.
  • Sharing: Vakat only passes on a weekly card, a verse or an invitation to the Circle when you tap “Share” yourself. You choose what is on it and who gets it. The app you share with processes it under its own rules.

8. Purchases and subscriptions

You buy Vakat Plus in the App Store. Apple handles the payment; Vakat never sees your payment details. Through StoreKit, Vakat learns on your device whether and which subscription is active, whether the free trial is running and when it renews. This decides whether Vakat Plus is open. The legal basis is the subscription contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); for purchase and payment, Apple’s privacy policy applies.

On Android, you buy Vakat Plus on Google Play. Google handles the payment; Vakat never sees your payment details. Through Google Play Billing, Vakat learns on your device whether and which subscription is active, whether the free trial is running and when it renews. Notices about your subscription, for example about a payment, and the request for a rating are shown by Google Play itself. Here too, the legal basis is the subscription contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); for purchase and payment, Google’s privacy policy applies.

Subscription statistics with RevenueCat: So that we can see how many subscriptions and trials there are, Vakat uses the service RevenueCat (RevenueCat, Inc., USA). For this, your device gets a random ID without a name or email address. RevenueCat receives:

  • your App Store purchases and subscriptions: product, price and currency, dates, trial, renewal, cancellation and refund
  • the country of your App Store, device model, iOS and app version, and your IP address
  • if you give them during setup: your age group, how you heard about Vakat and the figures you chose (woman or man), see section 9a

Apple also reports renewals, cancellations and refunds to RevenueCat directly (App Store Server Notifications), including for subscriptions that started before RevenueCat was added. RevenueCat never receives your prayers, pauses, your location, the Circle or the advertising ID; apart from the three details in section 9a, Vakat sets no attributes in RevenueCat. RevenueCat processes the data on our behalf in the USA; the transfer is safeguarded by standard contractual clauses. We keep purchase data as long as needed for the statistics and for tax obligations. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in evaluating our subscriptions (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can object at any time by email (section 12). RevenueCat’s privacy policy also applies.

Test with and without a free trial: New users of the iPhone app see the yearly plan with or without a free trial, at random. RevenueCat assigns this to the random ID so that we can see which offer works better; the statistics show per group how many subscriptions there are. The price and terms are always shown on the subscription page before you buy.

RevenueCat on Android: There RevenueCat receives the same information from Google Play: your Google Play purchases and subscriptions with the purchase token Google Play issues for each purchase, the country of your Google Play account, device model, Android and app version, and your IP address. Google reports renewals, cancellations and refunds directly to RevenueCat (real-time developer notifications). The automatic collection of device identifiers in RevenueCat is turned off; RevenueCat never receives the advertising ID.

Comeback price: If you’ve cancelled your subscription or it has ended, Vakat can show you a lower price for the first year. Vakat checks on your device, from your subscription with Apple, whether that’s possible; for Apple’s own win-back offer, Apple decides who gets it. Which offers you’ve already seen or used is remembered only on your device, including in the keychain, so an offer doesn’t start over after reinstalling. None of this goes to the usage analysis (section 9); RevenueCat receives a purchase at a comeback price like any other purchase. Notifications about offers only come if you turn them on in the app under “Me” → “Vakat Plus”; they are local and use no server. On Android, Vakat checks your subscription with Google Play to see whether a comeback price is possible; there is no win-back offer like Apple’s there. Which offers you’ve seen and which subscription you last had, Vakat remembers on Android only in the app’s own storage, without a keychain; after reinstalling, it starts over.

Signature for promotional offers: If you buy a comeback price through a promotional offer, Apple requires a signature from us. Vakat fetches it at that moment from our server at Google Cloud in Frankfurt am Main (europe-west3). Only the subscription and the offer are sent, no name, no identifier and no prayer data. Google processes your IP address to protect against abuse; we store none of it.

9. Usage analysis with Google Analytics

So that we can see where the setup gets stuck and how the subscription page works, Vakat uses Google Analytics for Firebase from Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland (together with Google LLC, USA). For this, Vakat sends:

  • which steps of the setup appear and are left, after how many seconds and with which button (continue, skip, not now, back), and that an answer was tapped on a question
  • answers that say nothing about your prayers: the language you chose, whether you entered a name (not which), how you found your place (suggestion, location or search, not which place) and whether you turned on reminders, the early reminder and the App Lock
  • on the subscription page: that it appears, which plan is selected, the tap on the purchase button and how the purchase ends, “Restore”, the links to the terms and the privacy policy and how long the page was open; plus the answer to “Should Vakat remind you?” before it
  • whether you chose the figures for women or for men during setup, as the property “figure”, so we can see whether the setup and the subscription page work equally well for both
  • if you answer the two questions: your age group and how you heard about Vakat, each as an event and as a property (“age_bracket”, “referral_source”), see section 9a
  • what Google Analytics records on its own: the first open, sessions and their length, and app updates

Never sent: your name, your city and your location, your prayer log, pauses, fasting, the Circle and your answers about your prayers (goal, which Asr, what we should support you with). For these questions it only counts whether you answered or skipped.

Google receives a random app instance ID, device model, iOS and app version, language and roughly your country and region, which are derived from your IP address; Google Analytics does not store the IP address itself. Vakat uses no advertising ID (IDFA), not the identifier for vendors (IDFV) and no Google advertising features. Google processes the data on our behalf, also in the USA; the transfer is based on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework, under which Google is certified. Google Analytics deletes the data after 14 months at the latest.

On Android, Vakat sends the same information; Google receives the Android version instead of the iOS version. On Android, too, Vakat uses no advertising ID (the permission for it is removed from the app) and no Google advertising features.

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in improving the setup and the subscription page (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can object at any time: in the app, turn off “Allow usage analysis” under “Me” → “Privacy”. Vakat then sends nothing more to Google Analytics and deletes the app instance ID on your device.

9a. About you: age, how you found Vakat, and figures

During setup Vakat asks how old you are and how you heard about Vakat (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, Google, friends or family, App Store or, on Android, Google Play, something else). You can skip both questions; then nothing is sent about them. You set your age on a wheel; only the age group is sent (13–17, 18–24, 25–34, 35–44 or 45+), and only if you turned the wheel.

If you answer, your age group and the way you heard about Vakat go to Google Analytics (section 9) and as attributes to RevenueCat (section 8). The same applies to your choice of the figures for women or for men. This information is tied to the respective random ID, never to your name or email address. Your exact age never leaves your device and is not stored. It shows us who Vakat reaches and through which channels, also for subscriptions; answers about your prayers are not part of it.

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in understanding who Vakat reaches (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can object at any time: if you turn off “Allow usage analysis” in the app under “Me” → “Privacy”, Vakat stops sending this information and deletes it at RevenueCat.

10. What Apple and Google share with us

In App Store Connect, Apple shows us aggregated figures, for example on downloads and purchases, without names or contact details. We only receive crash reports and usage statistics if you turned on “Share With App Developers” in the iOS settings. These do not tell us who you are either. We use them only to find and fix bugs (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Google Play shows us aggregated figures in the Play Console, for example on installs, purchases, ratings and the app’s stability, without names or contact details. We only receive crash reports and reports when the app stops responding (Android vitals) from devices on which you allowed usage and diagnostics data to be sent to Google. These do not tell us who you are either. We use them only to find and fix bugs (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

11. This website

This page is hosted on GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA). When you open it, GitHub stores your IP address for security reasons. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in providing the page securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). GitHub is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework; more in GitHub’s privacy statement. The page sets no cookies, loads no fonts or scripts from other servers and does not count visits.

12. Your rights

You have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection (Art. 15 to 21 GDPR). You can withdraw your consent at any time with effect for the future; for the Circle, directly in the app with “Withdraw consent”. Most data is only on your device, where you can view and delete it yourself. For anything else, write to david.zwirtmayr@dopaminedept.org. You can also request the deletion of your account there without the app (section 4b).

You can lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority, for example in the country where you live. In Austria, this is the Austrian Data Protection Authority (Datenschutzbehörde), Barichgasse 40–42, 1030 Vienna, dsb.gv.at.

You do not have to give Vakat any personal data; without the Circle, the app works fully. There is no automated decision-making and no profiling.

13. Changes

If Vakat changes in a way that processes different data, we will update this policy. The current version is always here. Should the Circle ever share more than it does today, Vakat will ask for your consent again first.
